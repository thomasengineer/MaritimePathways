Purpose & How to Use This Guide

This guide is a reference for entering or advancing in the U.S. maritime industry: where to find work by sector, how the licensed and unlicensed credentialing tracks work, which schools serve each track, and the tonnage/horsepower thresholds that gate each license level. It mirrors the companion spreadsheet workbook, organized here as a single scrollable reference with live links.

NMC (National Maritime Center) policy, sea-service formulas, and tonnage/HP breakpoints are periodically revised under Coast Guard NVICs and MMC policy letters. Treat the numeric thresholds in this guide as a study reference and confirm current figures at dco.uscg.mil/nmc before relying on them for a credential application.

Common breakpoints gating deck, engine, and towing vessel endorsements — verify current figures at NMC
Endorsement TypeThresholdApplies ToNotes

Engineer license power bandsUnder 4,000 HP / 4,000–6,000 HP / Over 6,000 HP (traditional); increasingly referenced in kW3rd/2nd/1st Assistant & Chief Engineer endorsementsHistorically horsepower-banded; NMC policy has moved toward propulsion power in kW for many equivalency determinations.
STCW III/1 vs III/2 engineer threshold~3,000 kW (approx. 4,023 HP) propulsion powerWatch scope: "small vessel" vs. full OICEW/Chief EngineerRelevant to ATB tug units — many large ATB tugs run 3,000–6,000+ kW, so aggregate tonnage/power equivalency of the tug-and-barge unit matters for crediting sea service toward III/2.
Towing vessel officer horsepower bandsUnder 1,500 HP / 1,500–4,000 HP / 4,000–6,000 HP / Over 6,000 HP (approximate, under 46 CFR Subchapter M)Uninspected & inspected towing vessels (tugs)Horsepower measured at rated continuous power of main propulsion engine(s); bands determine which towing vessel officer endorsement is required.
